## 2. Outbound vs inbound sales: key differences, pros, and when to use each
**Source:** Instantly Blog — https://instantly.ai/blog/outbound-vs-inbound-sales/  (fit score 5.0)
**Angle:** The real leverage in running both motions isn't just 'do both' — it's using inbound intent signals as triggers for outbound sequences, which turns warm interest into contacted pipeline before a competitor does.

**Linkedin**
```
The outbound vs. inbound debate misses the actual point.

Most B2B teams treat the two motions as separate programs with separate budgets and separate goals. The more interesting question is: what happens when an inbound signal — a pricing page visit, a webinar registration, a content download — automatically kicks off a targeted outbound sequence?

That's where the compounding effect lives. Not in picking a lane, but in letting inbound intent tell your outbound reps exactly who to call, when, and with what context. The sequence is no longer cold. The rep isn't guessing at fit. And the contact doesn't feel like a random interruption.

Per the Instantly Blog's breakdown of both motions (https://instantly.ai/blog/outbound-vs-inbound-sales/), teams running both outperform single-motion teams on revenue growth — which tracks, but the mechanism matters as much as the outcome.

If your team is generating inbound interest but outbound isn't picking it up, that's probably the gap worth closing first.
```
