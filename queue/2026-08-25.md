**Linkedin**
```
Clay and Apollo are often treated as competitors. In practice, they solve different problems for different team setups.

Apollo is a self-contained prospecting platform — data, sequences, and basic enrichment under one roof. Low friction to start, less flexible to customise. Clay is more of an enrichment and automation layer — you pull from dozens of data sources, run AI research steps, and wire it into whatever stack you already use. Higher ceiling, higher setup cost.

The real question isn't which one is better. It's whether your team has the time and appetite to build and maintain a data workflow, or whether you need something that just works out of the box. Most early-stage teams underestimate the maintenance side of a Clay build.

## 2. Account Agents now let you take action on account research in a workflow - The GTM with Clay Blog
**Source:** Clay Blog — https://www.clay.com/blog/account-agents-workflows  (fit score 2.4)
**Angle:** Account Agents in Clay workflows close the gap between research and action — but the real question is whether your GTM plays are structured well enough to benefit from the automation.

**Linkedin**
```
Most teams collect account research and then lose momentum before it becomes action.

Clay's new Account Agents — covered on the Clay Blog — let you trigger GTM plays directly from account research inside a workflow. The plumbing is genuinely useful: research surfaces a signal, and an agent acts on it without a human in the loop at every step.

But here's the part worth sitting with: automation accelerates whatever process you already have. If your account research criteria are fuzzy, or your sequences aren't calibrated to specific account contexts, the speed doesn't help much. The teams that will get the most out of this are the ones who've already done the work of defining what a good account looks like and what the right first move is when one shows up.

Are your outbound plays structured clearly enough to hand off to an agent — or is there still work to do on the underlying logic first?
