## 2. LinkedIn Outreach Benchmarks for Media Production (2026)
**Source:** Expandi Blog — https://expandi.io/industry-benchmarks/media-production/  (fit score 2.2)
**Angle:** Media production's rock-bottom LinkedIn response rates suggest the problem isn't the platform — it's that outreach in this space ignores how buyers in creative industries actually make decisions.

**Linkedin**
```
Media production sits at the bottom of LinkedIn outreach benchmarks — 38th out of 38 industries for message reply rate after acceptance, per the Expandi Blog's 2026 data.

That's a signal worth sitting with. It's tempting to read it as 'LinkedIn doesn't work for this sector.' But the more useful read: most outreach hitting media production buyers is built on assumptions that don't fit how those buyers operate. Creative decision-makers tend to buy on trust and referral. A cold message that leads with a feature list or a meeting request lands badly in that context.

If you're sending into this space, the sequencing and framing matter more than volume. A warmer signal before the first message — a comment, a share, a point of genuine relevance — changes the dynamic. Low benchmark averages often just mean the floor is easy to rise above.

## 3. LinkedIn Outreach Benchmarks for Transportation, Trucking & Railroad (2026)
**Source:** Expandi Blog — https://expandi.io/industry-benchmarks/transportation-trucking-railroad/  (fit score 2.2)
**Angle:** Transportation/trucking sits quietly in the middle of LinkedIn outreach performance — its connection-note reply rate is where the real edge hides, and most senders in the space aren't using it.

**Linkedin**
```
Transportation and trucking isn't usually the first industry people think of when discussing LinkedIn outreach performance. But it's quietly punching above average where it matters most.

Per the Expandi Blog's 2026 LinkedIn benchmarks, the sector ranks 6th out of 38 industries on connection-note reply rate (3.6% vs. a 3.0% platform average). Connection acceptance and post-acceptance reply rates are roughly in line with the norm — solid, not spectacular. That gap between 'accepted' and 'replied' is where most outreach in this vertical bleeds out.

What that suggests: decision-makers in freight, logistics, and rail are willing to engage when the opening note is relevant, but generic follow-up sequences kill the thread. The note is the real moment of leverage — not volume, not automation cadence.

If you're doing outbound into transportation or adjacent verticals, it's worth asking whether your connection note is actually doing any work, or just getting the foot in the door that nobody answers.
