## 1. LinkedIn Outreach Benchmarks for Telecommunications (2026)
**Source:** Expandi Blog — https://expandi.io/industry-benchmarks/telecommunications/  (fit score 2.2)
**Angle:** Telco's dead-last LinkedIn acceptance rate masks a surprisingly average reply-after-acceptance rate, which means the real problem is targeting and positioning before the connection request, not the conversation itself.

**Linkedin**
```
Telecommunications sits last out of 38 industries for LinkedIn connection acceptance — 21.8% against a 28.5% platform average. That's a rough number.

But here's the part worth sitting with: once someone actually accepts, telco reps get replies at 11.2% — slightly above the all-industry average of 10.4%. The conversation isn't the problem. Getting in the door is.

That gap usually points to one of two things: the ICP is too broad (sending to titles that have no reason to engage), or the connection note is doing too little work before the recipient even reads it. Most teams over-invest in message sequences and under-invest in the 30 words that decide whether a request gets accepted at all.

If you're running outreach into telco — or selling to it — the leverage is in the front end of the funnel, not the follow-up cadence.

## 2. LinkedIn Outreach Benchmarks for Food & Beverages (2026)
**Source:** Expandi Blog — https://expandi.io/industry-benchmarks/food-and-beverages/  (fit score 2.2)
**Angle:** Food & Beverage LinkedIn outreach has a hidden bright spot — post-acceptance reply rates — that most senders never exploit because they give up at the connection stage.

**Linkedin**
```
Food & Beverage sits near the bottom for LinkedIn connection acceptance (27.5%, just below the 28.5% platform average). On the surface, that looks like a tough vertical.

But look one step further: once someone accepts, F&B accounts get a 13.9% reply rate on follow-up messages — 3rd highest of 38 industries tracked, and well above the 10.4% average.

The implication is pretty straightforward. The problem isn't that F&B buyers are cold to outreach — it's that most sequences treat the connection request as the hard part and phone in the conversation that follows. If the warm-up message earns the accept, the follow-through message is where the real leverage is.

For anyone running outbound into food, beverage, or CPG accounts: optimise the post-accept message first, before you touch your connection note.
