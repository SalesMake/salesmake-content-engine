## 2. Clay vs 6sense: Choosing the Right Platform for ABM Campaigns - The GTM with Clay Blog
**Source:** Clay Blog — https://www.clay.com/blog/clay-vs-6sense  (fit score 4.8)
**Angle:** The Clay vs 6sense choice is less about features and more about whether your team needs predictive scoring or flexible data enrichment — and most outbound teams haven't asked that question clearly enough before buying.

**Linkedin**
```
Most ABM platform decisions get made backwards.

Teams see a vendor comparison, latch onto a feature list, and pick the tool that sounds most sophisticated. The real question — what does your outbound motion actually need right now — comes later, usually after an expensive contract is signed.

The Clay Blog published a solid breakdown comparing Clay and 6sense across intent signals, account scoring, orchestration, and pricing: https://www.clay.com/blog/clay-vs-6sense. Worth a read. The honest takeaway for most outbound teams: 6sense is built around predictive intent and ad targeting at scale; Clay is built around flexible data enrichment and workflow automation. Those are different bets on where your bottleneck actually lives. If your SDRs are drowning in unqualified accounts, a scoring layer might not fix it if the underlying data feeding that score is thin. And if you already have strong signal, you may not need another enrichment layer — you need orchestration.

Before the next renewal conversation or platform evaluation: can you name the single biggest friction point in your current outbound process? The answer usually tells you which category of tool you need.

## 3. The GTM Signal Your Competitors Can't Buy - The GTM with Clay Blog
**Source:** Clay Blog — https://www.clay.com/blog/how-first-party-signals-become-your-gtm-moat  (fit score 3.6)
**Angle:** First-party signals are valuable not because they're exotic, but because most teams never systematically surface what's already sitting in their CRM — and that gap is the real competitive edge.

**Linkedin**
```
Most teams are out shopping for better data when the edge is already in their own CRM.

A piece on the Clay Blog featuring Verkada's Cody Leovic makes a point worth sitting with: call transcripts, reply threads, and rep notes contain intent signals that no data vendor can replicate — because they came from your specific conversations, with your specific buyers.

The catch is that almost nobody builds a process to pull those signals back into targeting and sequencing. They stay buried in Salesforce fields nobody queries, or in Gong recordings nobody revisits. The result is that teams keep buying third-party signals to approximate something they already own in first-party form.

The practical question isn't "what signal should we add?" It's "what are we already capturing that we've never operationalised?"
