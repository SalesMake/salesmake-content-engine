## 1. How to build an AI-powered company research workflow for cold outreach
**Source:** Instantly Blog — https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-company-research-workflow/  (fit score 3.0)
**Angle:** AI research workflows are only as good as their validation logic — automating bad data faster is still bad data, so the quality gates matter more than the automation itself.

**Linkedin**
```
AI company research workflows sound like a shortcut. Sometimes they are. But the part most teams skip is the validation layer — the rules that decide whether enriched data is actually trustworthy before it touches your CRM or your sequences.

The Instantly Blog has a solid breakdown of how to structure a four-stage workflow: prompt, validate, sync, optimize. The 'optimize' piece is what I'd push people to take seriously. Measuring reply rates by data origin — knowing whether a signal came from an AI-scraped field, a manual entry, or a third-party enrichment tool — tells you which inputs are actually pulling weight. Without that, you're just automating volume and calling it a process.

If you're building something like this, the honest question to ask is: what's your fallback when the AI gets the company context wrong? A workflow without a human review gate for edge cases tends to erode trust in the data quietly, over time, until your reps stop using it.

## 3. AI sales agent vs manual prospect research: time, cost, and accuracy compared
**Source:** Instantly Blog — https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-sales-agent-vs-manual-research/  (fit score 2.0)
**Angle:** The real decision isn't AI vs. manual — it's knowing exactly where your ACV threshold is before you pick the method.

**Linkedin**
```
The AI-vs-manual research debate misses the more useful question: at what deal size does manual research actually pay for itself?

The Instantly Blog ran the comparison — per their numbers, AI agents handle hundreds of leads for under $0.10 each, while manual research runs 30–60 minutes per account. That math is straightforward at the extremes. Where teams get it wrong is the murky middle: mid-market accounts that feel enterprise-worthy but don't carry the margin to justify deep manual work.

A simple rule worth considering: set a hard ACV threshold. Below it, AI handles research and prioritization. Above it, a human reviews, adds context, and owns the outreach. The threshold should reflect your actual sales cost per hour, not gut feel.

What's the ACV number where your team flips from AI-first to human-first? Worth knowing before your next campaign build.
