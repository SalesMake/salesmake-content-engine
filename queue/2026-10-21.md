## 2. LinkedIn Outreach Benchmarks for E-Learning (2026)
**Source:** Expandi Blog — https://expandi.io/industry-benchmarks/e-learning/  (fit score 2.2)
**Angle:** E-learning's rock-bottom post-acceptance reply rate (37th of 38 industries) is the real story — acceptance is fine, but something in the follow-up message is killing the conversation.

**Linkedin**
```
E-learning sellers: your connection requests are landing. Your follow-up messages aren't.

Per the Expandi Blog's LinkedIn benchmark data, e-learning sits roughly at the platform average for connection acceptance (~28%). But post-acceptance message reply rate drops to 7.4% — nearly 3 points below the all-industry average and 37th out of 38 sectors tracked.

That gap matters. It means the problem isn't list quality or targeting. It's what happens after someone says yes to connecting. The opener, the timing, the assumed context — something in that first message is cooling people off fast.

A few things worth auditing if you're running outreach in this space:
→ Are you leading with a feature or a problem the prospect actually has?
→ Is the message arriving before the connection has had time to even notice you?
→ Does it read like you sent it to one person, or to a list?

Acceptance is just the door opening. What you say in the hallway is the whole game.

## 3. The 5 Stages of the B2B Buyer Journey in 2026 & How They Map to Your Sales Pipeline
**Source:** Expandi Blog — https://expandi.io/blog/b2b-buyer-journey/  (fit score 2.2)
**Angle:** B2B buying cycles reset every time a new stakeholder joins — outbound sequences need to account for that loop, not just a straight five-stage funnel.

**Linkedin**
```
Most outbound sequences are built like a straight line. But buying decisions rarely travel in one.

When a new stakeholder joins a deal mid-cycle, the group effectively restarts from Consideration or Evaluation — even if your contact thinks they're ready to decide. Expandi's breakdown of the modern B2B buyer journey makes this explicit, and it's worth sitting with: https://expandi.io/blog/b2b-buyer-journey/

The practical implication for outbound isn't just "map your touchpoints to funnel stages." It's that your sequences need to be built for re-entry — content and messaging that can re-establish context quickly when a CFO or legal lead drops into a conversation that's already been running for six weeks.

Static drip sequences don't handle that well. Neither does a single champion-focused approach when the real blocker is someone you've never spoken to.

Are your outbound workflows built for a single path through, or for the loop-backs that actually happen?
