## 2. LinkedIn Outreach Benchmarks for Electrical & Electronic Manufacturing (2026)
**Source:** Expandi Blog — https://expandi.io/industry-benchmarks/electrical-electronic-manufacturing/  (fit score 2.2)
**Angle:** The real signal in these benchmarks isn't the acceptance rate — it's that Electrical & Electronic Manufacturing ranks 6th out of 38 for post-acceptance reply rates, meaning the sector responds well once trust is established, which has direct implications for how you sequence and warm your outreach.

**Linkedin**
```
Most people benchmark LinkedIn outreach at the connection request stage. That's the wrong number to obsess over.

Per the Expandi Blog's 2026 industry benchmarks, Electrical & Electronic Manufacturing sits at a fairly average 29% connection acceptance rate — close to the all-industry mean of 28.5%. Unremarkable. But the post-acceptance reply rate tells a different story: 12.1% vs a 10.4% platform average, landing this sector 6th out of 38 industries tracked.

What that gap actually suggests: once someone in this space accepts a connection, they're more likely to respond to a follow-up message than contacts in most other industries. The bottleneck isn't openness — it's what happens in that first message after acceptance. Sequences that treat the follow-up as a formality leave a real response rate on the table.

If you're selling into manufacturing, the implication is straightforward: invest more in the content and timing of your post-acceptance touchpoint than in optimising your connection note.

## 3. LinkedIn Outreach Benchmarks for Medical Devices (2026)
**Source:** Expandi Blog — https://expandi.io/industry-benchmarks/medical-device/  (fit score 2.2)
**Angle:** Medical device outreach underperforms platform averages at every stage — the funnel leak is real, and fixing it starts with understanding why this audience is structurally harder to move.

**Linkedin**
```
Medical device LinkedIn outreach sits below the platform average at every stage of the funnel — connection acceptance, note replies, post-acceptance messages. Per the Expandi Blog's 2026 industry benchmark data, it ranks 30th or lower out of 38 industries tracked.

That's not surprising if you've worked this space. The buyers — clinical procurement leads, surgeons, hospital administrators — are highly gatekept, rarely on LinkedIn recreationally, and deeply skeptical of unsolicited contact. Generic sequences that work in SaaS or recruiting don't translate.

The practical implication: volume is the wrong lever here. Tighter ICP definition, relevance-first messaging, and longer patience windows matter more than send frequency. If your reply rate after acceptance is sitting below 9%, the problem is almost never the tool — it's the message-to-audience fit.
