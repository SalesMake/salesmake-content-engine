## 3. Inside Clay Workflows: The Story Behind Our New Building Primitive - The GTM with Clay Blog
**Source:** Clay Blog — https://www.clay.com/blog/the-story-of-clay-workflows  (fit score 2.4)
**Angle:** Moving orchestration logic out of tables and into dedicated workflows is a maturity signal — it's the difference between a clever hack and a system you can actually hand off or scale.

**Linkedin**
```
Clay Workflows aren't just a UI update — they're a structural shift in how GTM logic gets built.

For a long time, Clay tables did double duty: enrichment layer and orchestration layer at once. That works fine when one person owns everything. It starts to break when sequences get complex, when handoffs happen, or when you need to audit what fired and why. Separating the 'what to find' from the 'what to do with it' is the kind of architectural decision that looks minor until you're maintaining a system someone else built six months ago.

If you're designing outbound infrastructure in Clay, it's worth reading how the product team themselves frame the tradeoff — Clay Blog: https://www.clay.com/blog/the-story-of-clay-workflows
```
