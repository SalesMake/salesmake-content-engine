## 1. Less time on code, more time on architecture - The GTM with Clay Blog
**Source:** Clay Blog — https://www.clay.com/blog/code-reviews-agents  (fit score 2.4)
**Angle:** AI handling rote code review frees engineers to focus on architecture — the same logic applies to outbound: automate the mechanical, protect the judgment-intensive work.

**Linkedin**
```
Clay's engineering team just changed how they do code reviews. Instead of using AI to catch bugs line by line, they're using it to clear that work off the plate entirely — so engineers can focus on the decisions that actually require human judgment.

That distinction matters outside of engineering too. In outbound, most teams are still treating automation as a way to do the same mechanical tasks slightly faster. The more useful question is: which parts of the process genuinely require a person's judgment, and which parts are just friction in the way of getting there?

When you get that split right, the reps and strategists spend their time on sequencing logic, messaging decisions, and reading signals — not on list hygiene or copy-paste personalization. The volume takes care of itself.

## 2. AI sales agents for SDR teams: use cases that move meetings-set numbers
**Source:** Instantly Blog — https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-sales-agents-for-sdr-teams/  (fit score 2.0)
**Angle:** AI agents help SDR teams set more meetings by improving research quality and CRM discipline — not by simply increasing dial volume — and the rollout sequence matters more than the technology choice.

**Linkedin**
```
Most teams adopt AI sales tools and immediately point them at volume. More dials, more sequences, more noise. That's usually the wrong starting point.

The Instantly Blog published a useful breakdown of how AI agents actually move meetings-set numbers for SDR teams — and the sequencing they recommend begins with CRM hygiene and ramp support, not outreach automation. The logic holds: bad data fed through faster tooling just produces bad results faster. The framing around cost-per-meeting versus SQL quality is worth internalising too. Meetings-set is a vanity metric if the pipeline it feeds is weak. That's the conversation most teams aren't having when they evaluate AI tools — and probably should be.
