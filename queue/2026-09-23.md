## 2. What Is MCP, and Why Does Your Outbound Stack Need One?
**Source:** Expandi Blog — https://expandi.io/blog/mcp-for-outbound/  (fit score 2.2)
**Angle:** MCP matters for outbound not because AI can write messages, but because it can read live campaign state before acting — closing the gap between automation and actual context-awareness.

**Linkedin**
```
Most AI outbound tools are still operating blind.

They can draft a follow-up, but they don't know whether the connection request was accepted. They can personalize a message, but they can't check where the prospect actually sits in your sequence right now. That gap — between what the model knows and what's actually happening in your campaign — is where bad outreach slips through.

That's the problem MCP (Model Context Protocol) is designed to close. Instead of an AI agent working from static inputs, MCP lets it query live campaign data before taking any action. The Expandi Blog has a clear write-up on what this looks like in practice for LinkedIn outbound: https://expandi.io/blog/mcp-for-outbound/

The honest question for anyone running outbound at scale: how much of your automation is acting on stale context? Because the tooling is catching up to that problem faster than most teams realize.

## 3. LinkedIn Weekly Invitation Limit for 2026 (+ 5 Outreach Tips)
**Source:** Expandi Blog — https://expandi.io/blog/linkedin-connections-limit/  (fit score 2.2)
**Angle:** LinkedIn's fuzzy connection limits push practitioners toward quality-over-volume outreach — and most people are burning their weekly quota on the wrong prospects.

**Linkedin**
```
LinkedIn doesn't publish a hard rule, but the working consensus — covered well by Expandi Blog — is to stay under roughly 100 connection requests per week to avoid restrictions.

The limit itself isn't the real problem. The real problem is what most people fill that quota with: generic invites to anyone who vaguely fits a persona, sent in bulk, with no context. You've spent a scarce resource and created noise instead of a conversation.

A tighter weekly allowance is actually a forcing function: if you can only reach ~100 people, you should be able to say exactly why each one made the list. Relevance, timing, a specific reason to connect — those decisions belong before you hit send, not after you've burned through your allocation.

Are you treating your weekly invite budget like a limited resource, or burning through it and hoping something sticks?
