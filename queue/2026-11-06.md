## 2. AI sales agent meeting booking rate benchmarks 2026

**Angle:** The 12–15 meetings/month benchmark for AI agents is a planning assumption, not a performance guarantee — your actual rate is shaped by setup, targeting, and motion, so the more important habit is building a forecast from your own reply data.

**Linkedin**
```
AI agents booking 12–15 meetings a month sounds tidy. But that figure is a planning assumption, not a floor you can count on out of the box.

The more useful shift the Instantly Blog piece gestures at: stop treating meeting volume as the primary metric and start forecasting SQLs from reply rate data. Reply-to-SQL conversion is where your motion's real health shows up — before it shows up (or doesn't) in pipeline coverage numbers.

If you're building an AI-assisted outbound motion, the benchmark is a starting point for capacity planning, not a success target. Set it, then immediately instrument the layer underneath it. What's your reply-to-booked-meeting ratio looking like right now?

## 3. AI sales agent vs manual prospect research: time, cost, and accuracy compared
**Source:** Instantly Blog — https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-sales-agent-vs-manual-research/  (fit score 2.0)
**Angle:** The AI-vs-manual research debate is really a segmentation question: the right tool depends on deal size, not just volume.

**Linkedin**
```
The AI vs. manual research debate misses the actual decision.

It's not 'which is better' — it's 'which is right for this segment of your pipeline.' Per the Instantly Blog, AI agents can process hundreds of leads for under $0.10 each, while manual research runs 30–60 minutes per account. That gap matters, but the real lever is your ACV threshold.

Below that threshold, manual research is a margin problem. Above it, skipping manual review is a quality problem. Neither tool wins universally — the question is where in your funnel you've drawn the line, and whether that line is intentional or just inherited habit.

Most outbound teams we talk to haven't made that call explicitly. They default to one approach across the board and then wonder why conversion rates don't hold at scale (or why reps are buried in research on small deals).
